Sources, freshness & how to read these charts

Polling is vote share. Market prices are implied win probabilities. The two measures answer different questions and should not be interpreted as the same scale.

  • Silver Bulletin: published Datawrapper CSV feed; daily Democratic and Republican average, without reweighting. This is the headline average, not a separate likely-voter-only series.
  • FiftyPlusOne: public chart JSON, “All polls” series. Its commercial bulk API is separate and requires access.
  • Kalshi public REST API: Republican-party contracts for 2026. Current probability is the midpoint of a usable YES bid and ask; daily history is the closing bid/ask midpoint. Quotes without a usable two-sided book are excluded.
  • Polymarket Gamma API: Republican-party YES outcome price. Daily history comes from its CLOB prices-history API. Market rules, liquidity and sampling can differ between exchanges. Alaska’s Polymarket Democratic probability and history quote Mary Peltola’s YES contract directly; opposing-side values use its complement. Kalshi uses its Republican-party contract. Contract rules differ between the exchanges. Alaska’s official ballot identifies the candidates and parties.
  • DDHQ: daily Democratic and Republican estimates from the public 2026 generic-ballot chart (all-voter population mix). Methodology.
  • RealClearPolling: the publisher’s average as observed on its live page. Automated access may be blocked. Retained observations keep their original verification date and are labeled stale after a failed refresh. Full historical data is unavailable; a single observation is a dot, not an inferred trend.
  • Senate control: direct 2026 chamber-control contracts from each exchange, not a calculation from individual race probabilities. Kalshi uses CONTROLS-2026-R; Polymarket uses the Republican Party YES contract in “Which party will win the Senate in 2026?”. Democratic values use the opposite side. See each contract for tie-breaking, independent-caucus and settlement rules.
  • Selection: discover the 2026 U.S. Senate general-election contracts, match states with usable Republican quotes on both exchanges (Alaska uses Mary Peltola’s Democratic candidate contract), and sort by mean(|Kalshi − 50|, |Polymarket − 50|). Take the first ten. This avoids calling a large exchange disagreement a close race merely because its mean is 50. Independents are not counted as Democrats. Florida and Ohio are special elections.
  • Display: Senate values show the leading side’s full win probability: D 65% means a 65% Democratic chance and R 54% means a 54% Republican chance. A tied market reads 50–50. These are win probabilities, not vote-share margins. Blue denotes Democratic advantages; red denotes Republican advantages. Kalshi is solid and Polymarket is dashed. Nebraska uses I for an independent advantage.
    History: daily samples for up to 90 days, plus the latest quote. Missing history is never fabricated; a dot means only one observation is available. Tap, hover or focus a chart and use ← / → for values. Tap a legend swatch to hide a line; publisher names open the market. Drag across a chart to zoom; double-click to reset.
  • Freshness: retrieval times mean the API was queried then, not that a trade occurred then. Dates on ballot tiles are model dates. Old and failed sources are visibly labeled. A failed refresh retains the last successful data; it never generates sample values.
